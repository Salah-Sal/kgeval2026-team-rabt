\begin{abstract}
We describe Team Rabt's systems for both subtasks of the KnowledgeGraphEval
2026 shared task at ArabicNLP 2026. For Subtask~1 (AdaptNER), nested Arabic
NER under domain shift, we train a multi-head BIO tagger (one three-way
O/B/I head per entity type over a shared pretrained Arabic encoder) on
Wojood, ensemble three random seeds by span-level majority vote, and submit
predictions on the blind Konooz test slice spanning ten unseen domains. For
Subtask~2, forced-choice relation classification over 40 relation types
from WojoodRelations, we fine-tune a marker-based classifier and decode
with a type-constraint penalty derived from the training label inventory,
ensembled over five grouped cross-validation folds by logit averaging. Both
systems use no external data. Our submitted systems reached 0.7483 micro-F1
on AdaptNER (single model: 0.7385) and 0.9571 on relation extraction
(single model: 0.9519), 11.8 and 7.1 points above the official
baselines, respectively \final{leading the leaderboard at submission
time / final rank}. We analyze where the 18.6-point domain-shift tax on NER
falls (chiefly on recall), and show that type-constraint decoding and
ensembling are partially redundant remedies: the constraint penalty is
worth +1.3 points to a single RE model but only +0.1 on top of the
ensemble.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Knowledge-graph construction from Arabic text requires two capabilities in
sequence: finding the entities and typing the relations between them. The
KnowledgeGraphEval 2026 shared task decomposes this pipeline into two
subtasks. \textbf{AdaptNER} asks for nested named-entity recognition over
21 entity types, trained on the Wojood corpus and evaluated blind on
Konooz, a corpus deliberately drawn from ten domains unseen in
training, making robustness under domain shift, not in-domain accuracy,
the object of study. \textbf{Relation Extraction} asks for forced-choice
classification of a given entity-mention pair into one of 40 relation
types from WojoodRelations.

Our approach favors disciplined engineering of well-understood components
over novel architecture:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A faithful multi-head nested-BIO reimplementation} for
  AdaptNER: 21 independent per-type BIO heads on a shared encoder, which on
  in-domain Wojood test reaches 0.9240 span micro-F1.
\item \textbf{Type-constraint decoding for RE}: relation labels observed in
  training are indexed by (subject type, object type); at inference, logits
  of relations never observed for the pair's types are penalized. This is
  worth +1.3 points over the unconstrained single model.
\item \textbf{Ensembling matched to each task's variance source}: a
  random-seed ensemble with span-level vote decoding for NER (majority
  vote chosen over the union recall lever after a seed-agreement analysis
  showed the blind-set recall deficit to be systematic rather than seed
  variance) and grouped 5-fold logit averaging for RE, whose out-of-fold
  pooled predictions re-validate the single decode hyperparameter on the
  full training set.
\item \textbf{A leakage-free protocol}: the Konooz development split is
  never used for training, validation, or model selection (per task rules);
  every choice is made on Wojood-internal splits; and the systems run
  end-to-end on a single free-tier GPU ($\sim$90\,min NER, $\sim$68\,min
  RE), making the results cheaply reproducible.
\end{itemize}

Official results: AdaptNER micro-F1 0.7483 (precision 0.8060, recall
0.6982; single model 0.7385), RE micro-F1 0.9571 (single model 0.9519)
\final{final rank; keep pre-deadline probes separate if superseded}.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{The Wojood ecosystem.} Wojood introduced the 21-type nested
Arabic NER corpus used here (${\approx}550$K tokens and 75K mentions,
22.5\% of them nested), together with an AraBERT-based multi-task tagger
reaching 0.884 nested micro-F1 \citep{jarrar-etal-2022-wojood}. WojoodFine
refined four of its types with 31 ACE-style subtypes
\citep{liqreina-etal-2023-arabic}. Two shared tasks built on this lineage:
WojoodNER 2023, with flat and nested tracks (winning nested F1 93.73)
\citep{jarrar-etal-2023-wojoodner}, and WojoodNER 2024, whose Open
Track (fine-grained NER on a confidential out-of-domain test set, won at
73.7 F1) is the direct precursor of AdaptNER's cross-domain setting
\citep{jarrar-etal-2024-wojoodner}. WojoodRelations extends the ecosystem
to relation extraction: 33K sentences and ${\approx}15$K triples over 40
relation types sharing Wojood's entity scheme, with a supervised ArBERTv2
baseline at 88.61 micro-F1 that outperforms a GPT-4o RAG pipeline (85.78)
\citep{aljabari-etal-2025-wojoodrelations}. Konooz, the corpus behind the
AdaptNER blind test, spans 777K tokens from 160 sources across 10 domains
and 16 dialects under the same annotation guideline; its own benchmarking
shows Arabic NER models losing 12--20 F1 points across domains and up to
38\% out of distribution \citep{hamad-etal-2025-konooz}, precisely the
degradation this subtask is designed to measure.

\paragraph{Nested NER.} Three architecture families dominate: layered or
multi-level BIO decoding \citep{ju-etal-2018-neural,
katiyar-cardie-2018-nested}, span classification and biaffine span scoring
\citep{yu-etal-2020-named, yan-etal-2023-embarrassingly}, and unified
word--word formulations \citep{li-etal-2022-unified-w2ner}. On Wojood
specifically, the winning shared-task systems favor multi-task BIO decoding
over a shared encoder: muNERa (first place, nested 2024)
\citep{alotaibi-etal-2024-munera} and UM6P\&UL
\citep{mahdaouy-etal-2023-um6p}, whose unified imbalance-aware loss we
adopt. Our system follows this line with independent per-type BIO heads,
a formulation that cannot represent same-type nested pairs, which the
official 21-column submission format cannot encode either (and which Konooz
happens not to contain).

\paragraph{Marker-based relation classification.} Explicit argument
markers are the standard input encoding for sentence-level relation
classification: entity markers for relation representations
\citep{baldini-soares-etal-2019-matching}, typed markers
\citep{zhong-chen-2021-frustratingly}, levitated markers
\citep{ye-etal-2022-packed}. We use the punctuation-form typed markers of
\citet{zhou-chen-2022-improved}, which inject argument types without
expanding the vocabulary. Verbalization and entailment reformulations
\citep{sainz-etal-2021-label} target semantically close label inventories
such as ours; we instead exploit the task's type system directly, as a
decode-time constraint.

\paragraph{Arabic encoders.} AraBERT \citep{antoun-etal-2020-arabert},
ARBERT/MARBERT \citep{abdul-mageed-etal-2021-arbert}, CAMeLBERT
\citep{inoue-etal-2021-interplay}, AraELECTRA
\citep{antoun-etal-2021-araelectra}, and recently AraModernBERT
\citep{elshehy-etal-2026-aramodernbert} are the standard choices. Arabic
NER is notably sensitive to encoder choice (a ${\approx}14.5$ F1 spread
across variants in published Wojood-family results); AraBERTv02 remains the
consensus strongest for MSA and anchors both of our systems.

\section{Task and Data}

\paragraph{Subtask 1: AdaptNER.} Training data is Wojood
(${\approx}550$K tokens, ${\approx}75$K entity mentions, 21 entity types,
22.5\% of mentions nested \citep{jarrar-etal-2022-wojood}) with its
standard train/validation split. The blind test set is a slice of Konooz:
50,175 tokens across ten MSA domains released without labels; systems
submit one IOB2-tagged file with the domains concatenated in a fixed order
and are scored by nested span micro-F1. Task rules prohibit using any part
of Konooz (including the public development portion) for training,
validation, or model selection, and prohibit manual annotation of the test
set. Per the organizers, Konooz contains no same-type nested entities.

\paragraph{Subtask 2: Relation Extraction.} WojoodRelations provides
sentences with a marked subject and object mention; the system must output
exactly one of 40 positive relation types per instance (forced choice:
the test set contains no negative pairs, so abstention is not part of the
task and micro-F1 equals precision, recall, and accuracy). The distributed
training material contains 17,381 labeled records; the blind test set has
4,386 pairs. Official baselines \citep{aljabari-etal-2025-wojoodrelations}:
a supervised ArBERTv2 classifier at 88.61 micro-F1 and a GPT-4o RAG
pipeline at 85.78. For AdaptNER, the official no-adaptation AraBERTv02
baseline is 0.63 micro-F1.

\paragraph{Rules compliance.} Both of our systems were trained exclusively
on the distributed task data (no external corpora, no augmentation, no
distilled LLM outputs), so all reported configurations satisfy the shared
task's no-external-data reporting requirement for Subtask~2. All
predictions are produced fully automatically.

\section{System Overview}

\subsection{AdaptNER}

\paragraph{Architecture.} We fine-tune
\texttt{aubmindlab/bert-base-arabertv02} (the consensus strongest Arabic
encoder for MSA, matching the Wojood reference stack) with 21
independent per-type BIO heads: one three-way O/B/I classifier per entity
type, implemented as a single linear layer of width $21{\times}3$ over the
shared encoder states. The heads emit the 21-column submission format
directly, so no span-to-BIO conversion is ever needed; the only nesting the
format (and hence any competing architecture) cannot express is same-type
overlap, which Konooz does not contain.

\paragraph{Loss.} Following UM6P\&UL's unified imbalance-aware loss
\citep{mahdaouy-etal-2023-um6p}, each head is trained with
$0.4\cdot\mathrm{CE} + 0.2\cdot\mathrm{Dice} +
0.2\cdot\mathrm{Tversky}(\alpha{=}0.5) + 0.2\cdot\mathrm{Focal}(\gamma{=}2)$,
and the total loss is the mean over heads plus a variance penalty (weight
5.0) over per-head losses, which stops frequent types from dominating rare
ones. Loss terms are computed in fp32 under mixed precision, since the
Dice/Tversky batch-level reductions degrade in fp16.

\paragraph{Input.} Tokens are aligned by labeling only the first sub-token
of each word (the rest are masked); sentences longer than the 512-token
budget are split greedily at word boundaries with no overlap and
re-assembled by sentence and word offset after prediction. We apply no text
preprocessing beyond the encoder's own normalization.

\paragraph{Decoding.} Each head's tag sequence is decoded with standard
IOB2 rules; the single repair rule turns an orphan \texttt{I-x} (no open
span of type x) into the start of a new span, and every repair is counted
and reported.

\paragraph{Seed ensemble.} For our second submission we trained three
seeds (13, 42, 77) on Wojood train+validation combined, with a fixed
29-epoch schedule and no checkpoint selection: epoch 29 is where the
train-only baseline's validation curve peaked (flat between epochs 26--33),
and folding the validation split into training forfeits early stopping by
design. We decode three combination variants: \textbf{mean-prob} (argmax of
the seed-averaged softmax), \textbf{union} (keep any span predicted by
${\geq}1$ seed, a recall lever), and \textbf{majority vote} (${\geq}2$
seeds, a precision lever). Union and vote operate at span level:
predicted spans are counted across seeds, thresholded by votes, and
same-type overlaps among survivors resolved greedily by (votes, length,
position), since the output format cannot carry them. A fourth,
submission-side variant adds the original train-only model as a fourth
voter.

\subsection{Relation Extraction}

\paragraph{Architecture.} The same encoder
(\texttt{bert-base-arabertv02}) with typed punctuation entity markers in
the style of \citet{zhou-chen-2022-improved}: the subject mention is
wrapped as \texttt{@ * type * mention @} and the object as \texttt{\#
\textasciicircum{} type \textasciicircum{} mention \#}, injecting argument
types as plain text with no new vocabulary. The classifier concatenates the
encoder states of the two start markers and applies a two-layer
feed-forward head over 41 classes (40 relations + \texttt{no\_relation}).
When a mention string occurs more than once (5.9\% of instances) we mark
the disjoint occurrence pair with minimal distance; when the object is
embedded inside the subject mention (${\approx}20\%$), markers are nested
so both stay in context.

\paragraph{Label space.} We train on the full 41-way problem
(downsampling negatives is reported to cost ${\approx}10$ F1 in comparable
settings), but since the test set is forced-choice positive, the
\texttt{no\_relation} logit is set to $-\infty$ at decode time and never
emitted.

\paragraph{Argument typing at test time.} The blind test provides mentions
but not entity types, which both the markers and the constraints need. We
recover types by joining each record's sentence id against Wojood's gold
sentences and matching the mention string to that sentence's gold entity
surfaces (majority type on ties); records that fail the join fall back to a
corpus-wide mention-string$\rightarrow$type lexicon, and finally to UNK,
which degrades that instance to untyped markers and disables its
constraints rather than guessing. On the blind test, the sentence-id join
located the source sentence for 4,360/4,386 records (99.4\%); after the
lexicon fallback, 4,290 subjects (97.8\%) and 4,295 objects (97.9\%)
carried a type, leaving 96 subject and 91 object sides unknown.

\paragraph{Constraint decoding.} From the task's relation type
specification (domain/range of each relation) unioned with all (subject
type, object type) pairs actually observed in training (observation
extends the specification, never narrows it, since gold annotations
occasionally violate it), we build a table of inadmissible relations per
type pair. At decode, inadmissible relations' logits are penalized by a
fixed constant (a soft penalty, never a hard mask); a sweep over penalties
0--4 on development data selected 3.0.

\paragraph{Fold ensemble.} The final system trains five models on grouped
5-fold splits (grouping by sentence id, since sentences average 2.75
triples, and grouping prevents leakage; folds balanced greedily by record
count), each fold early-stopping on its own held-out fold with seed
13+fold. Test predictions average raw logits across the five models before
the penalty and mask are applied. The pooled out-of-fold predictions
(every training record predicted by the one model that never saw it)
re-validate the penalty choice on all 17,381 records rather than a single
2,174-record split. The averaged test logits and the constraint table are
exported as a small artifact, making any later penalty change a
seconds-long CPU re-decode instead of a GPU run.

\paragraph{Seed agreement on the blind set.} The union predicts only 8\%
more blind spans than the largest single seed (6,479 vs.\ 5,982, with 698
same-type overlap conflicts dropped), i.e., the three seeds largely agree
on the blind test. The recall deficit under domain shift is therefore
\emph{systematic} (entities no seed finds) rather than seed
variance, which caps what any within-architecture combination can recover
and argues for the precision-preserving vote over the union. The blind
score confirmed this: vote gained +1.9 precision but only +0.3 recall over
the single model (0.8060/0.6982 vs.\ 0.7874/0.6953).

The in-domain reference (0.9240) sits above the 0.884 nested F1 reported
by the original multi-head architecture paper
\citep{jarrar-etal-2022-wojood} \checkme{eval comparability before
claiming as improvement}.

\paragraph{The domain-shift tax.} In-domain Wojood test 0.9240 vs.\ blind
Konooz 0.7385: an 18.6-point drop with precision holding up far better
than recall (blind P$-$R gap: 9.2 points; in-domain the gap is $-1.4$,
i.e.\ recall was \emph{higher} in domain). Domain shift primarily costs
the model recall: entities in unseen domains are missed, not
mislabeled. This motivated the span-union ensemble as a candidate recall
lever for our second submission; the seed-agreement analysis above ruled
it out in favor of the precision-preserving vote, and the blind result
bore that out: the vote ensemble's +0.98 came almost entirely from
precision, while recall moved only +0.3; the recall deficit is
architectural, not recoverable by within-model combination.

Three external anchors put 0.7385 in context: it is 10.9 points above the
official no-adaptation AraBERTv02 baseline (0.63); it matches the winning
score of WojoodNER-2024's Open Track (73.7), the closest published
analogue of this cross-domain setting \citep{jarrar-etal-2024-wojoodner};
and the 18.6-point drop sits at the top of the 12--20-point cross-domain
degradation band that the Konooz corpus paper itself measures for Arabic
NER models \citep{hamad-etal-2025-konooz}.

\paragraph{Constraint decoding and ensembling are partially redundant.}

penalty sweep (0--4, Table~\ref{tab:sweep}) is monotone and flat near the
top; we keep 3.0, which maximizes macro-F1.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What we deliberately did not do.} Our design phase surveyed and
rejected several families on evidence rather than taste. For nested NER:
LLM in-context extraction (published Arabic NER results of 0.29--0.74 F1,
and fine-tuned 7B models at or below BERT-base), MRC-style per-type
querying ($21{\times}$ inference cost), and grid/word--word decoders whose
distinguishing power (same-type nesting) the 21-column output format
cannot express anyway. For RE: LLM-only prediction (the task's own GPT-4o
RAG baseline trails the supervised baseline by ${\approx}3$ points
\citep{aljabari-etal-2025-wojoodrelations}), negative downsampling
(${\approx}10$ F1 cost in comparable settings), and hard constraint
masking: gold annotations occasionally violate the type specification,
so constraints must stay soft.

\paragraph{The zero-shot condition, quantified in advance.} Our design
notes projected ${\approx}0.73$ micro-F1 for a Wojood-only system
transferred zero-shot to Konooz, before any target-domain adaptation; the
submitted single model scored 0.7385. The adaptation levers we designed
but did not build within the deadline are span-masked continued
pretraining on the unlabeled test tokens (transductive,
organizer-sanctioned), guarded self-training, and organizer-endorsed LLM
domain-targeted augmentation. These were projected to be worth a further
3--8 points; they are the obvious next step, and all are legal under the task
rules (the Konooz ban covers labels, not the released unlabeled test
tokens; confirm this reading with organizers before any post-deadline
claim).

\paragraph{Constraint decoding vs.\ ensembling.} As Table~\ref{tab:redev}
shows, the two remedies overlap almost entirely on micro-F1 but not on
macro-F1: after logit averaging, the penalty's residual value is
concentrated in rare relations. A practical recipe for forced-choice RE
with a type system: ensemble first, then constrain, and expect the
constraint to matter mainly in the tail.

\section{Limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item The per-type BIO-head architecture cannot represent nesting of two
  same-type entities. Konooz happens to contain none, so this costs
  nothing here, but the architecture is not a general nested-NER solution.
\item No external data, augmentation, or domain-adaptive pretraining was
  used; our numbers are a floor for what the architecture family achieves.
\item All decisions were validated on Wojood-internal splits; with no
  labeled out-of-domain validation set permitted, the choice among
  ensemble variants for the blind set rested on in-domain scores and the
  seed-agreement analysis alone. Only two of the ten submission slots were
  used, so the rejected variants remain unmeasured on the blind set.
\item Single encoder family per task; no cross-architecture ensembling.
\item Development metrics use our own exact-match span scorer and
  positives-only micro-F1 implementation, reconciled against submission
  rehearsals but not against the official evaluation code; only Codabench
  numbers are authoritative.
\item RE argument typing at test time is a heuristic join against Wojood
  gold entities; on the blind test 96 subject and 91 object sides could
  not be typed and ran with untyped markers on that side and constraints
  disabled.
\end{itemize}
