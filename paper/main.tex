\begin{abstract}
We describe Team Wasl's systems for the KnowledgeGraphEval 2026 shared
task at ArabicNLP 2026, which decomposes Arabic knowledge-graph
construction into two evaluated stages: nested NER under domain shift and
relation extraction between given mentions. For Subtask~1 (AdaptNER), we
train a multi-head BIO tagger (one three-way O/B/I head per entity type
over a shared pretrained Arabic encoder) on Wojood and combine three
random seeds by span-level majority vote. For Subtask~2, forced-choice
classification over the 40 \WojoodRel{} relation types, we fine-tune a
typed-marker classifier with type-constraint decoding, ensembling five
grouped folds by logit averaging. Our submissions scored 0.7483 micro-F1 on
AdaptNER's blind Konooz slice (ten unseen domains), 4th of the eight
officially ranked teams and 11.8 points above the official baseline, and
0.9571 micro-F1 on relation extraction with the no-relation class masked
at decoding, 2nd at the results release; re-scored by the organizers
over all 41 classes, the same ensemble reaches 0.9328, 4th in the
organizers' final ranking. We analyze where
the 18.6-point domain-shift tax on NER falls: chiefly on recall. We
further show that type-constraint decoding and ensembling are partially
redundant (+0.9 points on a single RE model, +0.1 on the ensemble) and
that the surviving errors share an argument-type signature that no type
constraint can separate.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Knowledge-graph construction from Arabic text requires two capabilities in
sequence: finding the entities and typing the relations between them. The
KnowledgeGraphEval 2026 shared task \citep{JHJ2026} decomposes this
pipeline into two subtasks. \textbf{AdaptNER} asks for nested
named-entity recognition over 21 entity types, trained on the Wojood
corpus and evaluated blind on Konooz, ten domains unseen in training.
\textbf{Relation Extraction}
asks for forced-choice classification of a given entity-mention pair into
one of 40 relation types from \WojoodRel{}. We entered both subtasks
as Team Wasl and finished 4th and 2nd of the eight officially ranked
teams at the results release (\S\ref{sec:official} reports the
organizers' later re-scoring of Subtask~2).

Our approach favors disciplined engineering of well-understood components
over novel architecture: (1)~a faithful reimplementation of the multi-head
nested-BIO recipe for AdaptNER; (2)~type-constraint decoding for RE, which
penalizes relation logits inadmissible for the pair's argument types;
(3)~ensembling matched to each task's variance source,
random seeds with span-level voting for NER and grouped 5-fold logit
averaging for RE; and (4)~a leakage-free protocol, under which Konooz never
touches training, validation, or model selection.

\section{Related Work}

Our systems assemble published components of the Wojood ecosystem rather
than new architecture. Wojood supplies the 21-type nested Arabic NER
corpus and the
multi-task BIO reference tagger \citep{jarrar-etal-2022-wojood}, extended
by two WojoodNER shared tasks \citep{jarrar-etal-2023-wojoodner,
jarrar-etal-2024-wojoodner}, whose 2024 out-of-domain Open Track is
AdaptNER's direct precursor. \WojoodRel{} adds 40 relation types over
the same entity scheme \citep{aljabari-etal-2025-wojoodrelations},
\WojoodHad{} extends the annotation layer to event arguments
\citep{aljabari-etal-2024-event}, \textit{WojoodOntology} places the
entity and relation types in an ontology aligned with Schema.org and
Wikidata \citep{aljabari-etal-2025-wojoodontology}, and Konooz, the corpus behind the blind
test, measures 12--20 F1 points of cross-domain degradation for Arabic
NER models \citep{hamad-etal-2025-konooz}. From the wider literature we
adopt the multi-head per-type BIO
formulation with the unified imbalance-aware loss of
\citet{mahdaouy-etal-2023-um6p}, typed punctuation entity markers for
relation classification \citep{zhou-chen-2022-improved}, and AraBERTv02
\citep{antoun-etal-2020-arabert}, the encoder of the organizers' official
baselines for both subtasks.

\section{Task and Data}

\paragraph{Subtask 1: AdaptNER.} Training data is Wojood with its
standard train/validation split. The blind test set is a slice of Konooz
released without labels: 50,175 tokens of MSA across its ten
domains. The full corpus also spans sixteen dialects, which the task
excludes, so the shift studied here is domain shift within MSA, not
dialect shift. Systems
submit one IOB2-tagged file and are scored by nested span micro-F1. Task
rules prohibit using any part of Konooz, including its public
development portion, for training, validation, or model selection.

\paragraph{Subtask 2: Relation Extraction.} \WojoodRel{} provides
sentences with a marked subject and object mention; the system outputs
exactly one of 40 positive relation types per instance. The task page
states that the test set contains no negative pairs, under which micro-F1
equals precision, recall, and accuracy; the official protocol, clarified
after the results, scores the full 41-class output, so a
\texttt{no\_relation} prediction counts as a miss
(\S\ref{sec:official}). The distributed training material
contains 17,381 labeled records; the blind test set has 4,386 pairs. Both
systems train only on the distributed task data (the Subtask~2 reporting
requirement), and all predictions are automatic.

\section{System Overview}

\subsection{AdaptNER}

\paragraph{Tagger.} We fine-tune
\texttt{aubmindlab/bert-base-arabertv02} with 21 independent per-type
O/B/I heads (a single $21{\times}3$-wide linear layer over the shared
encoder states), which emit the 21-column submission format directly.
Following \citet{mahdaouy-etal-2023-um6p}, we train each head with
$0.4\,\mathrm{CE} + 0.2\,\mathrm{Dice} +
0.2\,\mathrm{Tversky}(\alpha{=}0.5) + 0.2\,\mathrm{Focal}(\gamma{=}2)$,
and the total loss adds a variance penalty across per-head
losses to stop frequent types from dominating rare ones; its weight, 5.0,
is the value \citeauthor{mahdaouy-etal-2023-um6p} fix without tuning, and
we did not tune it either (sensitivity: Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}). We label only
the first sub-token of each word and split over-long sentences at word
boundaries within the 512-token budget. Decoding applies standard IOB2 rules with a single
repair (an orphan \texttt{I-x} starts a new span).

\paragraph{Seed ensemble.} The submitted ensemble trains three seeds (13,
42, 77) on train+validation, with a fixed 29-epoch schedule,
the train-only model's validation peak; folding validation into training
forfeits early stopping. We combine the seeds in
three ways: \textbf{mean-prob} (argmax of the seed-averaged softmax),
\textbf{union} (any span predicted by ${\geq}1$ seed, a
recall lever), and \textbf{majority vote} (${\geq}2$ seeds, a precision
lever); overlap resolution: Appendix~\ref{app:stats}.

\subsection{Relation Extraction}

\paragraph{Classifier.} We pair the same encoder with typed punctuation
entity markers in the style of \citet{zhou-chen-2022-improved}: the
subject mention is wrapped as \texttt{@ * type * mention @} and the
object as
\texttt{\# \textasciicircum{} type \textasciicircum{} mention \#},
so the argument types enter as plain text with no new vocabulary; a
two-layer feed-forward head over the concatenated start-marker states
classifies into 41 classes (40 relations +
\texttt{no\_relation}). We train on the full 41-way problem. Our official
submission set the \texttt{no\_relation} logit to $-\infty$ at decoding,
following the task page's statement that the test set holds no
negatives; at the organizers' request after the results we resubmitted
the same ensemble with the mask removed (\S\ref{sec:official}).

\paragraph{Argument typing at test time.} The blind test provides
mentions but not the entity types the markers and constraints need. We
join each record's sentence id to Wojood's gold sentences (entity
annotations only, never relation labels) and match the mention string
to a gold entity surface, falling back to a corpus-wide
mention$\rightarrow$type lexicon and finally to UNK, which disables that
instance's constraints rather than guessing (coverage:
Appendix~\ref{app:stats}).

\paragraph{Constraint decoding.} We build a table of inadmissible
relations per (subject type, object type) pair from the task's relation
type specification unioned with every type pair observed in training,
since gold annotations occasionally violate the specification. At decode we subtract a
fixed constant from each inadmissible relation's logit, a soft penalty
rather than a hard mask; a sweep over penalties 0--4 on development data
selected 3.0.

\paragraph{Fold ensemble.} The final system averages the raw test logits
of five models trained on grouped 5-fold splits, grouped by sentence id
because sentences average 2.75 triples and row-level splits would leak;
each fold early-stops on its own held-out fold. The pooled out-of-fold
(OOF) predictions re-validate the penalty choice on all 17,381 records.

\section{Experimental Setup}

All experiments ran on a single NVIDIA T4 (free Kaggle tier) with
deterministic seeds; no reported system cost more than
${\approx}4$ GPU-hours in aggregate. Appendix~\ref{app:hyper} lists the
hyperparameters; code is available at
\url{https://github.com/Salah-Sal/kgeval2026-team-rabt}.

\paragraph{Validation protocol.} AdaptNER model selection used only the
Wojood validation split (early stopping on span micro-F1); we scored the
Wojood test split once per finished system as an in-domain reference;
Konooz never entered any loop. For RE, we made development decisions on a
single grouped held-out split (15,207 train / 2,174 dev), replaced for
the final system by grouped 5-fold cross-validation.

We used two submission slots per track: v1 single models and v2
ensembles (Table~\ref{tab:manifest}).

\subsection{Development results and analysis}
\label{sec:dev}

\paragraph{Ensembling where the variance is.} In domain, majority vote
is the strongest seed combination: +0.6 over the ensemble's own seed-13
member (train+val, 0.9268; Table~\ref{tab:nerdev}), the reference for
all in-domain ensemble deltas; the union, the intended recall lever, buys
+0.9 recall for $-2.0$ precision. On the blind set the three seeds largely
agree (the union holds only 8\% more spans than the largest single
seed), so the recall deficit under shift is \emph{systematic}: entities
no seed finds. We therefore submitted the precision-preserving vote, and
the blind result bore the reading out: +1.9 precision, +0.3 recall over
the submitted single model (v1).

\paragraph{The domain-shift tax falls on recall.} The submitted single
model (v1: train only, early-stopped seed 13) drops 18.6 points from its
in-domain Wojood test score (0.9240) to blind Konooz (0.7385), a domain
shift within MSA,
and the drop falls chiefly on recall ($-23.6$ points, vs.\ $-13.0$
precision), reversing the in-domain P--R profile
(Table~\ref{tab:nerdev}). Two anchors put 0.7385 in context:
10.9 points above the official no-adaptation baseline, and at the top of
the 12--20-point
degradation band Konooz itself measures \citep{hamad-etal-2025-konooz}.

\paragraph{Constraint decoding and ensembling are partially redundant.}
The type-constraint penalty is worth +0.92 points on the single model but
only +0.08 pooled-OOF on top of the 5-fold ensemble
(Table~\ref{tab:redev}): most type-inadmissible errors are single-model
flukes that logit averaging already removes. The penalty's remaining
value concentrates in macro-F1 (+1.0 point OOF), i.e., in rare relations,
which is why we selected the penalty on macro-F1 rather than on the
official micro-F1: micro-F1 peaks at 4.0, by 0.0001, and the whole sweep
spans 0.0008 (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}). The recipe: ensemble first, then
constrain.

\subsection{Error analysis}
\label{sec:errors}

\paragraph{RE: the surviving errors share type signatures.} We reran one
fold model of the submitted ensemble on its held-out fold (3,476
records), leaving 74 misclassified positives, and their structure
explains why the constraint penalty saturates: the residual confusions
hold between relations with the \emph{same} argument-type signature,
which no type constraint can separate by construction. Two of the largest
clusters are PERS--GPE \texttt{lives\_in} vs.\ \texttt{president\_of} (11)
and GPE--GPE \texttt{capital\_of} vs.\
\texttt{geopolitical\_division} (7). Two patterns recur. First, a pull
toward the type pair's majority relation even against an explicit
lexical cue: in \arx{برنامج القدس الفلسطينية عاصمتنا} (``the program
`Palestinian Jerusalem, Our Capital'\,''), gold \texttt{capital\_of} is
predicted as \texttt{geopolitical\_division}, which outnumbers it
59-to-1 in training. Second, genuine overlap
between readings: for
\arx{رئيسة وزراء بريطانيا العظمى، مارغريت تاتشر} (``the Prime Minister
of Great Britain, Margaret Thatcher''), the model outputs
\texttt{president\_of}, the closest leadership relation the inventory
offers, while
gold encodes \texttt{lives\_in}; the confusion runs both ways (6 vs.\ 5
cases), unlike the one-way majority pull
above. On the blind test the penalty flips only 6 of 4,386
ensemble predictions.

\paragraph{NER: metonymy, attachment, and register.} On Wojood test
(0.9240), errors partition into four mutually exclusive categories
(Appendix~\ref{app:stats}). Exact-boundary type
confusions number only 138, and 60\% of
them are ORG$\leftrightarrow$GPE metonymy: in \arx{أنتجت بولندا بنشاط
البنادق الهجومية} (``Poland actively produced assault rifles''),
\arx{بولندا} is gold-annotated ORG as a state actor but predicted GPE,
its default type. Same-type boundary errors (510) concentrate on
trailing locatives and appositions, e.g.\ gold \arx{نافورة تابيثا}
(``the Tabitha Fountain'') predicted with \arx{في يافا} (``in Jaffa'')
attached. Outright misses (591) and spurious spans (876) both skew
toward NORP, ORG, and EVENT, and misses reach into colloquial-register
occupations such as \arx{شاويش} (``sergeant'').

\paragraph{Blind-set behavior.} Without blind gold, we can characterize
out-of-domain errors only behaviorally. Seed unanimity over predicted spans orders the ten domains
by distance from the training register: spans predicted by a single seed
account for 13--15\% of all predicted spans in Finance and Economics,
the registers closest to Wojood's news-heavy composition, but 28\% in
Science and Sport (Table~\ref{tab:domains}).

\subsection{Post-submission loss ablation}

After the submission close we ablated the unified loss
(Appendix~\ref{app:ablation}; no run touched Konooz or a scored
submission). The result is negative: plain cross-entropy slightly
outperforms the submitted recipe in domain (Table~\ref{tab:ablation};
single-seed runs), and partial mixtures collapse rare types.
The variance penalty functions as a guard against the instability the
other loss terms introduce (+12.4 macro over the penalty-free mixture),
not as a lever over CE.

\section{Conclusion}

Disciplined engineering of published components produced 4th of 8 on
nested NER under domain shift and 2nd of 8 on relation extraction at
the results release, 4th of 7 after the organizers' 41-class re-score:
one Arabic encoder, two task-shaped heads,
ensembling matched to each task's variance, and a leakage-free protocol. The clearest
finding is negative space: the domain-shift tax falls on
recall and is shared across seeds, so closing it requires
target-domain adaptation, not better combination of Wojood-only
models (design alternatives: Appendix~\ref{app:design}).

\section{Limitations}

The per-type BIO-head architecture cannot represent nesting of two
same-type entities; Konooz happens to contain none, so this costs nothing
here, but the architecture is not a general nested-NER solution. We used
no external data, augmentation, or domain-adaptive pretraining, so our
numbers are a floor for this architecture family; each task also relies
on a single encoder family. With no labeled out-of-domain validation
permitted, the choice among ensemble variants rested on in-domain scores
and the seed-agreement analysis alone, and the rejected variants remain
unmeasured on the blind set. Development metrics use our own scorer
implementations, reconciled against submission rehearsals but not the
official evaluation code; only Codabench numbers are authoritative. RE
argument typing at test time is a heuristic join against Wojood gold
entities; 96 subject and 91 object sides could not be typed and ran with
untyped markers and constraints disabled. No result carries a confidence
interval or significance test. Seed variance on the blind set is
unmeasured, since each seed was submitted only inside the ensemble, so
the +0.98 ensemble gain there is a single-seed comparison; in domain, the
three seeds span 0.20 points (Table~\ref{tab:nerdev}).

\section{Prediction Statistics}
\label{app:stats}

\begin{itemize}
\item RE marker placement: when a mention string occurs more than once
  in its sentence (5.9\% of instances), we mark the disjoint occurrence
  pair with minimal distance; when the object is embedded inside the
  subject mention (${\approx}20\%$), markers are nested so both stay in
  context.
\item AdaptNER seed combination: for union and vote, same-type overlaps
  resolve greedily by (votes, length, position).
\item AdaptNER blind test: the v1 single model predicts 5,780 spans over
  50,175 tokens (density 11.5\%, close to Wojood's), all 21 types emitted,
  201 BIO-sequence repairs during decoding; the submitted vote ensemble
  predicts 5,670 spans (Table~\ref{tab:domains}). Health yields the fewest
  predictions in both (228).
\item RE blind test: the ensemble changed 97/4,386 predictions (2.2\%)
  vs.\ the single model, concentrated in the
  \texttt{located\_in}/\texttt{subsidiary} and
  \texttt{president\_of}/\texttt{lives\_in} confusion clusters, the
  same affiliation/location clusters that dominate development-set
  confusion matrices.
  34/40 relation types appear in the ensemble output;
  \texttt{located\_in} alone accounts for 46\%.
\item RE test-time typing: the sentence-id join located the source
  sentence for 4,360/4,386 records (99.4\%); with the lexicon fallback,
  4,290 subjects (97.8\%) and 4,295 objects (97.9\%) received a type; the
  remaining 96 subjects and 91 objects were typed as unknown, disabling
  their constraints rather than guessing.
\item NER error taxonomy (\S\ref{sec:errors}): each errant gold span is
  paired with at most one errant predicted span, by precedence: exact
  boundaries with a different type (confusion), then same type with
  overlapping boundaries (boundary). Unpaired gold spans count as
  misses, unpaired predicted spans as spurious, so the four categories
  are mutually exclusive by construction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Design Alternatives Considered}
\label{app:design}

The design phase rejected the following on published evidence rather
than taste: LLM
in-context NER (published Arabic results of 0.29--0.74 F1), MRC-style
per-type querying ($21{\times}$ inference cost), grid decoders whose
extra expressiveness the submission format cannot carry, LLM-only RE
(the task's GPT-4o RAG baseline trails its supervised one
\citep{aljabari-etal-2025-wojoodrelations}), negative downsampling
(reported to cost ${\approx}10$ F1 in comparable settings), and hard
constraint masking (gold annotations occasionally violate the type
specification, so constraints must stay soft). Our design notes
projected ${\approx}0.73$ micro-F1 for zero-shot
Wojood$\rightarrow$Konooz transfer; the submitted single model scored
0.7385. The adaptation levers designed but not built by the deadline
(continued pretraining on the unlabeled test tokens, guarded
self-training, domain-targeted augmentation) were projected to be worth
a further 3--8 points and are the obvious next step.

\section{Post-Submission Loss Ablation and Penalty-Weight Sweep}
\label{app:ablation}

Run August 3, after the July 29 submission close, on the AdaptNER
baseline configuration (Wojood-only, seed 13); no ablation run touched
Konooz, the leaderboard, or any submitted system. Adding Dice to CE
collapses PRODUCT to 0 F1; the full mixture without the variance penalty
additionally zeroes UNIT and QUANTITY; the variance penalty rescues all
three. Whether E1's slight in-domain edge holds under domain shift is
untestable without Konooz gold. A sweep of the penalty weight (P1, P2,
P10; run September 4 after the reviews, same setup) shows macro-F1
rising monotonically from 0 to 5.0 and falling back at 10.0, while
micro-F1 varies by at most 0.1 points from 0 to 5.0 and drops 0.4 at
10.0; on this single seed the untuned 5.0 is the best of the five
weights on macro-F1 and no worse than any other on micro-F1.
